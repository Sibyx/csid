Two scopes, because a capture is a mixture on two axes. An ambient channel interleaves several PHY types at once, and an illuminated one interleaves a metronomic injector with whoever else is talking. The spectrum, phase, impulse-response, Doppler and metronome views are scoped to one class and one transmitter — mixing geometries changes the array widths under the plots, and mixing transmitters produces timing statistics that belong to none of them. The waterfall can still show every class, placed by frequency. Nothing is dropped from the capture: csiscope only reads the live stream, and every record still lands in capture.raw.

The second chain cancels CFO/SFO by conjugate multiplication before the Doppler transform. Without it the spectrum smears.

What you are looking at

A Wi-Fi radio in monitor mode reports, for every frame it decodes, the channel state information: one complex number per subcarrier per antenna pair, describing how the air between the transmitter and this node changed that frame. csiscope reads that stream live and draws it. It never touches the capture — every record still lands in capture.raw whatever this page shows.

Three things decide whether a plot on this page means anything. Read these before reading the panels.

1. A record is not a measurement

The driver can deliver a frame with an intact header, the right payload length, a plausible signal strength — and every coefficient equal to zero. It is not a very weak channel. It is the absence of a reading. Measured across four nodes of this fleet on 23 August 2026, one record in six arrived that way.

csiscope removes those records from every windowed view and tells you how many it removed. That is the empty tile in the header strip and the records used line in the Capture panel. The capture yield beside it now counts only records that measured something, so it reads lower than it used to and it is the number that was always meant.

2. Everything is scoped to one class and one transmitter

A busy channel interleaves several PHY types packet by packet — legacy 52-tone, HT 56-tone, HE 242-tone — and their arrays are different widths. A plot that mixes them is not a measurement of anything. An illuminated channel interleaves a metronomic injector with whoever else is talking, and pooling their arrival times produces timing statistics that belong to neither.

So the spectrum, phase, impulse response, Doppler and metronome panels all describe one record class from one transmitter, chosen in the rail on the left. The census that fills those selectors is taken before the scope is applied, so choosing one transmitter can never hide the others from you. The waterfall can still show every class at once, placed by frequency.

3. Amplitude is relative, phase is not the channel

|H| carries the receiver's own automatic gain control. Two frames of an unchanged channel can differ by several decibels because the radio changed its gain, not because anything moved. Read shape off an amplitude axis, never absolute power. The only absolute anchor on this page is the per-chain RSSI, which has its own panel.

Raw phase is dominated by carrier and sampling frequency offsets, which have nothing to do with the room. The sanitised view fits a straight line across the subcarriers and subtracts it — and that removes any genuinely linear part of the channel with it, including the common flight time of every path. The residual is a change indicator, not a delay.

Panel by panel

Capture
What csid says about itself, which the live stream cannot report. The important line is the yield: records that measured something, over frames the radio delivered. A low yield on 2.4 GHz usually means DSSS/CCK traffic — frames with no OFDM preamble, so nothing to report a channel estimate from. That is a busy channel, not a quiet one.
Metronome
Is this transmitter periodic? If a rate was commanded, the panel compares what arrived against it and the difference is the delivery deficit. If no rate was commanded, the slot is inferred from the arrivals themselves — and when they turn out not to be periodic at all, the panel says irregular and reports no deficit. A deficit against a rate nobody commanded is not a measurement.
Waterfall
Amplitude in dB, subcarrier across, time down, newest at the top. This is the panel to watch for motion: a person walking through the link draws bright and dark banding that sweeps across the subcarriers. A still room draws vertical stripes that do not move. The readout says how many rows reached the display and how many the frame rate could not carry.
Spectrum & bundle
The current frequency response, over the 5th–95th percentile envelope of the window behind it. The width of that envelope is the crowd-sensing feature: a static channel gives a tight bundle, motion widens it.
Doppler spectrogram
Time across, frequency shift up and down. Motion towards the receiver shifts the reflected path up in frequency, motion away shifts it down, and the panel converts that to a radial speed using the tuned centre frequency.

Read the CV first. A Doppler axis assumes evenly spaced samples, and packets arrive when somebody transmits. The coefficient of variation of the gaps says how far from even they were: below about 0.5 the axis is quantitative, above 1.2 treat it as a picture rather than a measurement. The fill figure says what share of the frequency analysis had no sample near it and was filled in. To make this panel trustworthy, throttle the capture to a fixed rate.

The frequency axis is deliberately held still across columns, even when the packet rate moves, so that the left of the image can be compared with the right.

Phase
Phase across the subcarriers, in one of three views. Raw is what the radio reported and is dominated by offsets. Unwrapped undoes the ±π wrapping. Sanitised subtracts the fitted straight line and is the only one worth reading for shape — see point 3 above.
Impulse response
The same channel seen as delay rather than frequency: each peak is a path, and later peaks are longer paths — reflections.

Delay here is relative. The radio starts its analysis at a packet boundary it detected, and that detection moves from packet to packet, so the whole profile slides. The panel therefore centres it on the strongest tap and the axis runs negative to positive around that.

Smooth does not mean detailed. Resolution is set by bandwidth alone: two paths closer together than 1/B are one tap. On a 20 MHz capture that is about 60 nanoseconds — roughly 18 metres of path difference. The panel draws that interval so you can see how much of the curve is interpolation.

Complex plane
Every subcarrier's complex value plotted as a point, coloured by subcarrier index. A clean capture traces a smooth arc or spiral; a scatter with no structure means the extraction is wrong.
Chains
One trace per receive/transmit antenna pair. They should differ — they are different antennas seeing different geometry. Traces that lie exactly on top of each other mean the parser is reading the same bytes twice, which is what the chains are distinct check tests.
Subcarrier time series
Three subcarriers followed across the window. This is the raw material of every occupancy feature: motion in the room appears here as correlated wobble across all three.
RSSI
Received signal strength per chain, in dBm. The one absolute amplitude on the page. Below about −80 dBm the channel estimate is noisy enough that fine structure elsewhere should be distrusted.
Inter-arrival
How the gaps between this transmitter's frames are distributed, on a logarithmic axis because they routinely span three decades. One narrow mode means a metronome. Two modes means bursts: a spacing inside a burst and a much longer gap between bursts. Two modes are why an inferred slot cannot be trusted, and why the Doppler axis needs the CV beside it.
Clock divergence
The host clock and the firmware clock, each minus the baseband clock, across the window. Steady lines mean the three agree; a step means one of them jumped, which matters when captures from several nodes have to be aligned afterwards.
Band plan
Where Bluetooth Low Energy's advertising channels fall inside this capture's subcarriers, for coexistence work. On a 5 GHz capture it reports that BLE cannot appear at all, which makes the capture a clean negative control.
CSI ratio
One chain divided by another. The two share a radio and therefore share the frequency offsets, so those divide out exactly and what is left is a ratio of two air channels. This is the trick behind FarSense and Widar3's Doppler processing, and it is why the Doppler panel wants a second chain selected.
Subcarrier statistics
Per-tone median level and how much each tone varied over the window. Tones that never vary carry no information; tones that vary a lot carry the motion. The band is not flat, and this shows which parts of it are worth using.
Who is sounding the channel
Every transmitter in the window, busiest first. On a passive capture the record rate is somebody else's transmit rate, so this table is the honest answer to "why is the rate what it is". Click scope to point every deep view at one of them.
Everything on the channel
The record classes present, as tone count and modulation. Click one to scope the deep views to it. On an ambient channel there are usually several; on an illuminated one there is often exactly one, and then the transmitter selector is the axis that matters.
Extraction checks
Is this really CSI? Adapted from the "Free Your CSI" tests. These catch a driver or parser that is handing over something other than a channel estimate — wrong dimensions, duplicated chains, a stream of zeros. A check that cannot apply to this tone grid says so rather than failing.
Stream health
The path between csid and this page. It is best-effort by construction: a slow browser drops frames here and the capture is unaffected. Sender-side drops and decode errors both rising means the node is loaded, not that the radio is failing.

Reading the controls

window
How many records the windowed statistics look back over. Longer is smoother and slower to respond.
frame rate
How often this page redraws. It costs CPU on the capturing node; lower it if the capture matters more than the view.
waterfall rows/frame
How many records each redraw may draw. The stream can far exceed the frame rate, and this decides how much of it you actually see. The readout says what was skipped.
dB floor / ceiling
The range the waterfall's colours span. auto-scale to signal fits them to the current window.
chain / pair with
Which antenna pair the single-chain views use, and which second one the Doppler view divides by. Leaving the pair unset leaves the Doppler spectrum contaminated by carrier offset.
centre frequency
Pins the frequency used to turn Doppler shift into speed. Inference from the channel number cannot resolve 6 GHz, whose channel numbers overlap 2.4 GHz, so pin it when it matters.

This console cannot change anything

There is no configuration editor, no start or stop button, no export trigger. Arming a capture is done by merging a plan; configuration is managed by Ansible; the archive is read by the tools that own it. This page reads the live socket and the node's own status file, and that is all it can do.
